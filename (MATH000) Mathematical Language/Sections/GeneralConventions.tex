\section{General Notation and Conventions}

Prefer parentheses for ordinary grouping when delimiters are interchangeable, including in nested expressions. Use other delimiters when established mathematical notation calls for them. Use the adopted standard arrow forms and the usual sizes of delimiters and separators by default. Adjust their lengths or sizes only when there is a clear mathematical or typographical reason.

In LaTeX mathematical source, put one source-space after punctuation commas. TeX determines the typeset spacing; retain explicit spacing commands only where typographically appropriate.

\begin{ruletable}
Scalars & Ordinary mathematical italic letters, for example $a, b, c, \lambda, t$. \\
Variables and Labels & Use mathematical italic for variables and indices, and upright Roman type for descriptive labels and abbreviations, unless established mathematical notation calls for otherwise. \\
Vectors and Matrices & Vectors and matrices are written in bold, typically with lowercase and uppercase letters, respectively. Greek symbols are also bold when used for vectors or matrices. Vectors are columns by default; established field-specific conventions are respected. \\
Matrix Delimiters & Parentheses are preferred; square brackets are a standard alternative. \\
Dimension Subscripts & Use dimension subscripts when informative; omit them when the dimension is clear or irrelevant \\
Number Systems & Blackboard-bold letters, such as $\N, \Z, \Q, \R, \C$ \\
Fields & Arbitrary fields are typically denoted by italic uppercase letters such as $F, K, L$. Established blackboard-bold notation, such as $\mathbb{F}$ and $\mathbb{F}_q$, is also used where appropriate. \\
Sets & Ordinary uppercase letters such as $A, B, X, Y$, unless a field-specific convention requires otherwise. \\
Families and Collections & Calligraphic letters, such as $\mathcal{F}$; script letters, such as $\mathscr{A}$, represent distinct notation \\
Symbol Distinctions & Different mathematical letterforms are treated as distinct symbols, such as $A$, $\mathbf{A}$, $\mathcal{A}$, $\mathscr{A}$, $\mathbb{A}$, $\epsilon$, $\varepsilon$, $\phi$, and $\varphi$. \\
Named Operators & Upright Roman type, for example $\det$, $\ker$, $\im$, $\rk$, and $\tr$. \\
Large Operators & Use display-style large operators and conventional index placement; use compact forms only when the layout requires them \\
\end{ruletable}

\begin{notationtable}
$(a_1, \dots, a_n)$ & Ordered tuple \\
$(a_n), \ \{a_n\}$ & Sequence \\
$(a_i)_{i\in I}, \ \{a_i\}_{i\in I}$ & Indexed family over $I$ \\
$(a, b)$ & Open interval \\
$[a, b]$ & Closed interval \\
$[a, b)$ & Left-closed, right-open interval \\
$(a, b]$ & Left-open, right-closed interval \\
$\{a_1, \dots, a_n\}$ & Set delimiters \\
$\begin{aligned} &\{x\in A\mid P(x)\}, \\[0.05em] &\{x\in A\colon P(x)\} \end{aligned}$ & Set-builder notation \\
$\langle x, y\rangle$ & Pairing or inner product, according to context \\
$|x|$ & Absolute value, modulus, cardinality, or order, according to context \\
$\|x\|$ & Norm \\
$a=b$ & Equality \\
$a\coloneqq b$ & $a$ is defined to be $b$. \\
$a\equiv b$ & Identity, equivalence, or congruence, according to context \\
$a\cong b$ & Isomorphism or congruence, according to context \\
$a\approx b$ & Approximation \\
$f\colon X\to Y$ & Function or map from $X$ to $Y$ \\
$x\mapsto f(x)$ & Elementwise rule of a map \\
$f\colon X\to Y, \ x\mapsto f(x)$ & Function with domain, codomain, and mapping rule \\
$g\circ f$ & Composition: first $f$, then $g$ \\
$f^{\circ n}$ & $n$-fold composition of a self-map $f$ \\
$f|_A, \ f\restriction_A$ & Restriction of $f$ to $A$ \\
$\id_X$ & Identity map on $X$ \\
\end{notationtable}
